An efficient appliance is not simply the model with the biggest green badge. The right choice fits your household, uses less energy for the required job and does not include oversized features you will rarely use. Energy labels can help, but they must be read in context.

<Tip title="Lifecycle Cost vs Sticker Price">
An appliance that costs 15% more upfront but has a superior energy rating often saves several times that price difference over a 10-year lifespan.
</Tip>

## Compare annual energy use

Where labels show estimated annual consumption, compare models of similar size and type. A very large efficient refrigerator may still use more electricity than a smaller model that meets your needs.

## Use the official label for your country

Energy-rating systems differ by region. Look for the current official label and understand what the scale means. Older labels or screenshots from another country may not be comparable.

## Size matters

Buy capacity that matches your household. Oversized fridges, freezers, air conditioners and water heaters can waste energy or cost more than necessary. Undersized equipment can also perform poorly if it runs constantly.

## Refrigerators and freezers

These run all day, so efficiency matters. Compare annual kilowatt-hour use, total capacity and features such as ice makers, which can increase energy use. Check whether the layout fits the way you shop.

## Washing machines

Look for efficient water and energy use at the loads you actually wash. High-spin speeds can reduce dryer time, but fabric care still matters. Eco cycles are often slower because they trade time for lower energy.

## Dishwashers

Efficient dishwashers can use less hot water than hand washing large loads, especially when run full. Compare energy and water use, and check whether the rack layout suits your dishes.

## Dryers

Heat-pump dryers can use substantially less electricity than conventional electric resistance dryers, but purchase price and drying time differ. Compare lifetime running cost if you use a dryer frequently.

## Air conditioners and heat pumps

Efficiency metrics vary by market, but higher seasonal efficiency generally means less electricity for the same heating or cooling output. Correct sizing and professional installation are essential.

## Standby and smart features

Connected features can be useful for scheduling or monitoring, but they are not automatically energy-saving. Check standby consumption and decide whether the feature solves a real problem.

## Calculate lifetime cost

Estimate purchase price plus expected energy cost over the years you plan to own the appliance. An efficient model may justify a higher upfront cost if the usage is high and the savings are meaningful.

## Maintain what you buy

Cleaning filters, keeping vents clear, checking seals and following maintenance guidance protects efficiency. Even the best appliance performs poorly if it is dirty, blocked or incorrectly installed.

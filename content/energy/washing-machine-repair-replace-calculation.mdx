Compare a washing-machine repair quote with the complete replacement cost, then add running-cost differences over the same period. Do not replace a repairable machine merely because a new model has a better energy label. Equally, a low repair quote is not useful if safety, parts availability or recurring faults make the repair unsuitable.

This worksheet supports an ordinary household purchase decision. It is not a fault diagnosis, a current price survey or a prediction of how long either machine will last.

## Deal with safety and ownership first

Stop using a machine with suspected electrical damage, burning smells or another serious fault and obtain appropriate help. [Electrical Safety First's washing-machine advice](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/products-and-appliances/kitchen-appliances/washing-machines/) includes checking recalls and safe operation. Do not open the casing to gather figures.

If the appliance belongs to a landlord, report the fault and clarify responsibility before commissioning work or buying a replacement. Check warranties, repair cover and any relevant consumer advice separately; this worksheet does not determine legal entitlements.

## Collect comparable quotes

For repair, ask what the price covers: diagnosis, call-out, parts, labour, VAT where applicable, repeat visits and any guarantee. Ask what remains uncertain. “Possible pump fault” is not the same offer as an assessed repair with a specified part.

For replacement, include purchase, delivery, installation, required accessories and lawful removal of the old unit. Check dimensions and access. A machine that does not fit the space is not a valid comparison, however attractive the headline price.

Our [appliance selection guide](/blog/energy-efficient-appliances-guide) helps with broader suitability, including capacity and actual use.

## Put both options on the same time horizon

Use these illustrative formulas:

**Repair option = repair cost + expected operating cost during the chosen period.**

**Replace option = installed replacement cost + operating cost during the same period.**

Add future repair allowances only as clearly labelled scenarios, not as known facts. Include water costs if they differ materially and you have a relevant metered tariff. Keep finance costs separate and include them if applicable.

Suppose a repair is £150 and a complete replacement is £500. Replacement has a £350 upfront premium. If it saves an illustrative 0.25 kWh per comparable cycle at 25p/kWh, the energy saving is 6.25p per cycle. At 200 cycles annually, that is £12.50 a year. Energy savings alone would take 28 years to recover £350, before other differences. These inputs are examples, not market averages or a lifespan forecast.

## Read energy figures correctly

The [UK energy-label service documentation](https://create-energy-label.service.gov.uk/api-documentation/endpoint/Washing%20machines%20and%20washer-dryers) identifies washing energy figures in kWh per 100 cycles. Divide that figure by 100 for a label-based per-cycle comparison. Standardised label conditions may differ from your programmes and loads.

For example, a label value of 50 kWh per 100 cycles corresponds to 0.5 kWh per labelled cycle, not 50 kWh each wash. Compare like-for-like test figures; do not compare a washer-dryer's combined wash-and-dry figure with washing alone.

For measurement limitations, see our [plug-in monitor guide](/blog/plug-in-energy-monitor-rented-flat). Only measure where appliance and monitor instructions permit it.

## Test uncertainty rather than hide it

Run three scenarios: repair holds for the chosen period; another paid repair is needed; replacement also incurs an unexpected cost. Keep the assumptions visible. If one option looks preferable only under a very optimistic lifespan, say so.

Consider downtime, laundrette access, repair availability and suitability for your household. Environmental impacts are not captured by electricity cost alone. The [Energy Saving Trust maintenance discussion](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/how-save-energy-when-using-your-washing-machine) supports extending useful life through care; follow your own machine's maintenance instructions rather than a universal schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a fixed age when replacement becomes better?

No. Condition, diagnosis, parts, use and costs differ.

### Should I replace whenever repair exceeds half the purchase price?

That shortcut omits installation, remaining-life uncertainty and suitability. Use complete quotes instead.

### Can a better energy rating pay for the machine quickly?

Calculate with realistic consumption and use. A rating improvement alone does not establish a short payback.

### What if I cannot get a firm repair quote?

Record the diagnostic charge, likely range and uncertainty. Compare that range rather than inventing a single cost.

### Must water savings be included?

Include relevant costs where you can substantiate them. Otherwise label them unquantified rather than assuming zero difference.

## Make the decision reviewable

Write down the diagnosis, complete quotes and three uncertainty scenarios. Choose a suitable safe option based on that record. Keep the paperwork so later repairs or replacement decisions can use actual history rather than a remembered headline price.

Microgreens are young edible seedlings harvested shortly after the first leaves develop. They are popular because they grow quickly, need little space and can fit on a kitchen shelf or windowsill. A successful first crop depends on clean trays, suitable seed, even moisture and enough light.

<Tip title="Sanitation & Airflow">
Microgreens need adequate air circulation and clean trays to prevent damping off and mold. Never let trays sit in stagnant water after the initial blackout germination phase.
</Tip>

## Choose seeds intended for growing

Use untreated seed from a reputable source, ideally sold for sprouting or microgreen use. Popular beginner choices include radish, broccoli, mustard, pea shoots and sunflower. Different crops germinate at different speeds, so grow one variety per tray at first.

## Use a shallow tray with drainage

A shallow growing tray is easier to manage than a deep pot. You can use a clean seed tray with drainage holes placed inside a solid catch tray. Fill it with a thin layer of clean seed-starting mix or another medium recommended for microgreens.

## Sow evenly, not deeply

Microgreens are usually sown more densely than mature garden plants. Spread seed as evenly as you can so seedlings do not form thick clumps. Press them gently onto the moist surface and follow the seed supplier's guidance about whether the variety should be lightly covered.

## Keep germination evenly moist

During germination, the goal is moisture without waterlogging. A light mist can help settle small seeds, while bottom watering becomes useful once roots are established. Avoid leaving trays in standing water for long periods.

## Give seedlings enough light

After germination, move the tray to strong light. A bright window can work for some crops, but a simple grow light often produces straighter, more even growth. If seedlings are stretching and leaning heavily, they likely need stronger or more balanced light.

## Improve airflow

Dense seedlings stay humid, so gentle airflow helps leaves dry and can reduce problems. Do not blast the tray with a strong fan. Keep the growing area clean and avoid crowding multiple wet trays tightly together.

## Water from below when possible

Once roots reach the bottom of the growing layer, adding water to the lower tray can keep leaves drier. Pour away excess water rather than leaving the roots submerged. The exact frequency depends on temperature, tray size and crop.

## Know when to harvest

Many microgreens are harvested when the first true leaves begin to appear, though timing varies. Use clean scissors and cut above the growing medium. Harvest only healthy growth and discard anything with an unusual smell, visible mold or signs of decay.

## Store and use them simply

Freshly cut microgreens are best used quickly. If you wash them, dry them thoroughly before refrigeration because excess moisture can shorten storage life. Add them to sandwiches, salads, eggs, grain bowls or soups just before serving.

## Reset the tray after harvest

Most microgreen trays are treated as a single crop. Compost or discard the spent root mat, wash the tray thoroughly and start again. Keeping the process clean between batches is one of the easiest ways to improve consistency.

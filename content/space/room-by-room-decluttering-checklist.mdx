Decluttering is easier when the project has a boundary. Instead of trying to 'declutter the house,' work through a short list of rooms and stop when the planned area is complete. This weekend plan is designed to create useful progress without turning your home upside down.

<Tip title="One Room at a Time">
Avoid tearing apart multiple rooms simultaneously. Completing one focused zone creates momentum and provides an immediate sanctuary space.
</Tip>

## Before you begin: prepare four destinations

Use bags or boxes for Keep Here, Move Elsewhere, Donate/Sell and Recycle/Discard. The most important rule is to remove donation and rubbish bags promptly. Otherwise, they become a new form of clutter.

## Entryway

Remove shoes that are damaged or never worn, old receipts, empty bags and items that belong elsewhere. Keep only the current daily essentials near the door. Create a small landing zone for keys, wallets and outgoing items.

## Kitchen

Start with expired food and duplicate utensils. Check mugs, storage containers and cookware for items you never choose. Group food by type and put older products where you will see them first. Leave counter space for the tasks you actually do every day.

## Living room

Remove items that migrated in from other rooms. Review magazines, cables, decorative objects and blankets. Give remotes and chargers a permanent home. Keep surfaces edited enough that they can be reset quickly.

## Bedroom

Clear the floor and bedside area first. Review clothing that no longer fits your life, not just your body. If you have several similar items, keep the ones you reach for. Make space for laundry so worn clothes do not become a chair pile.

## Bathroom

Discard expired or unusable products according to local guidance. Combine duplicates where safe and sensible, and keep daily products accessible. Store backups separately so you know what you already own.

## Workspace

Sort paper into action, archive and discard. Remove cables that no longer match devices. Keep only the supplies you use regularly at arm's reach. A clear work surface makes it easier to start focused tasks.

## Digital spaces

Delete duplicate downloads, clear the desktop, remove unused apps and unsubscribe from low-value email lists. Create a simple folder system rather than dozens of categories. The goal is to find things, not to create perfect digital filing.

## Sentimental items

Do these last. Decision-making is easier after you have practiced on low-emotion categories. Keep meaningful items that you genuinely value, but give them a deliberate storage or display location.

## End with a reset

Take out recycling, place donations by the door or in your car, return relocation items to their rooms and clean the newly cleared surfaces. Photograph the finished areas if that motivates you. Then stop. Maintenance is more useful than exhausting yourself.

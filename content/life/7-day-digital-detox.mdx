A digital detox does not have to mean disappearing from the internet. For most people, a short reset works better when essential communication, work and navigation remain available. This seven-day plan focuses on reducing automatic use and replacing it with deliberate choices.

<Tip title="Physical Alarm Clock">
Replace your phone alarm with a standalone bedside clock. Keeping your smartphone outside the bedroom prevents morning and late-night scrolling cycles.
</Tip>

## Day 1: Measure without judging

Check your device's screen-time report and note the three apps that consume the most unplanned time. Also notice how often you pick up the phone. Do not try to fix everything yet. The goal is to understand the pattern.

## Day 2: Remove notification noise

Disable non-essential alerts, badges and marketing notifications. Keep the ones that protect safety, security or important relationships. A quieter device reduces the number of times your attention is pulled away without your choice.

## Day 3: Redesign the home screen

Move high-distraction apps away from the first screen. Keep tools such as maps, camera, calendar, messages and authentication where they are easy to reach. If a distracting app is still necessary, make it slightly less convenient to open.

## Day 4: Create one phone-free zone

Choose one recurring place or activity where the phone is not used: meals, the bedroom, the bathroom, family time or a daily walk. Put the device physically out of reach rather than relying only on willpower.

## Day 5: Replace one scrolling window

Identify your most predictable scrolling period and plan an alternative in advance. Read ten pages, take a walk, stretch, cook, water plants, call someone or do a small household task. Replacement gives your brain somewhere else to go.

## Day 6: Try a low-input block

Spend two or three hours without social feeds, news, podcasts or short-form video. Essential calls and practical tools can remain available. Notice the urge to fill silence. Let some moments stay quiet.

## Day 7: Design your long-term rules

Review what felt easiest and what made the biggest difference. Choose two or three rules to keep, such as no phone for the first 30 minutes of the day, social apps checked twice daily, or charging the phone outside the bedroom.

## What to do when work requires screens

Separate productive screen use from recreational scrolling. A laptop used for focused work is not the same as constant feed checking. Use full-screen apps, close irrelevant tabs and take breaks that do not involve another screen.

## Avoid all-or-nothing thinking

If you spend an evening scrolling, the detox has not failed. Return to the next planned boundary. The goal is to create a better default, not to prove perfect self-control.

## Make boredom useful again

Short moments of boredom can lead to reflection, conversation or creative thought. You do not need to fill every queue, elevator ride or five-minute break with content.

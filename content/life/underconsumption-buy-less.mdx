Underconsumption is a simple idea: use what you already own more fully before buying something new. It has become a visible online trend, but the useful part is not the aesthetic. It is the habit of slowing down purchases, reducing duplication and separating genuine needs from temporary excitement.

<Tip title="The 72-Hour Rule">
When tempted to make an impulse online purchase, add it to a wishlist and wait 72 hours. In over 70% of cases, the emotional urge to buy subsides completely.
</Tip>

## Start with what you already have

Before shopping, check the category at home. If you want a new notebook, skincare product, kitchen tool or shirt, look at what is already available. Often the need is real, but the purchase can wait until something is finished or worn out.

## Use a 72-hour pause

For non-essential purchases, save the item and wait three days. The delay removes some of the urgency created by ads and social media. For expensive items, wait longer and compare alternatives.

## Track duplicates, not every possession

You do not need a full home inventory. Focus on categories where repeat buying happens: cables, water bottles, cosmetics, cleaning products, storage boxes, basic clothing or kitchen gadgets.

## Finish open products first

Use-one-open-one is a useful rule for consumables. Keep one shampoo, cleaner or pantry staple in active use before opening the backup. This reduces half-used products and makes it easier to know when replacement is actually needed.

## Repair before replacing when practical

A loose button, worn zip or small furniture issue may be cheaper to repair. Repair is not always economical, but checking the option creates a useful pause before automatic replacement.

## Buy secondhand for suitable items

Furniture, tools, books, sports equipment and some clothing can be good secondhand purchases. Inspect condition and safety. For products where hygiene, structural integrity or current safety standards matter, new may be more appropriate.

## Unfollow purchase triggers

If certain accounts consistently make you feel that your clothes, home or devices are inadequate, mute or unfollow them. Your feed is part of your shopping environment.

## Create a replacement list

Keep a short list of items that genuinely need replacing. When a product reaches end of life, you already know the next priority. This is more intentional than browsing for upgrades.

## Spend more selectively when quality matters

Buying less does not always mean buying the cheapest option. For frequently used items, durability, repairability and comfort may justify a higher price. The aim is lower churn, not lower standards.

## Do not romanticize scarcity

Many people consume less because of limited income, not lifestyle choice. Underconsumption should not become a moral ranking system. Use it as a tool for your own decisions, not a reason to judge someone else's needs.

## Measure success by use

A successful purchase is something that serves you repeatedly. A successful no-purchase decision is one you forget about a week later. Both reduce clutter and make spending more aligned with real priorities.
